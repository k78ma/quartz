- What specific changes/additions do you notice between the four iterations of SSE? 
- Do you agree with the additions, or would you have gone in a different direction? 
- Was it necessary to make the attraction more entertaining than educational to maintain its relevance? Think about which scene was your favourite and why. What would you add or remove? Using all you've learned from this course; do you believe SSE succeeded as an attraction based on the history of communication?

Between the four versions of Spaceship Earth, it gradually shifted from a serious, museum-like history lesson to something more emotional, and audience-friendly. The 1982 version seems the most educational of the four. It was described as more of a “museum piece,” with no background music, a very academic tone. Then the 1985 version lightened that approach with Walter Cronkite’s narration, more wit, and the addition of music, while also making the ending less tied to specific predictions and more timeless. By 1994, the ride had to respond to the growing importance of computers, the internet, and the “global neighbourhood,” which makes sense because communication history had changed so quickly. The 2007 version seems like the most immersive update, with upgraded animatronics, a new Judi Dench narration, a refreshed finale, and interactive touchscreens in each ride vehicle. 

I mostly agree with those additions. Communication technology changes so fast that an attraction like SSE almost has to evolve or it risks becoming a time capsule of outdated future predictions. I especially think the 1985 decision to make the finale more timeless was smart, because very specific predictions can age badly. The later additions of internet history and interactivity also seem necessary. That said, I do think something may have been lost as the ride became more entertaining. The original version sounds like it took the original mission of EPCOT most seriously, and that is part of what made Spaceship Earth unique in the first place. I probably would not have pushed it too far toward spectacle; there is a balance between staying engaging, but still feeling grounded in the actual history of communication, rather than just becoming another futuristic Disney ride. 

I do think it was probably necessary to make the attraction somewhat more entertaining in order to stay relevant to general audiences. Most theme park guests are not looking for a lecture, even if they are open to learning something. The addition of music, warmer narration, updated visuals, and interactive elements likely helped keep people emotionally invested. Still, I do not think it needed to become less educational to succeed. The best version of Spaceship Earth is probably one that balances both goals.

My favorite scene is probably the transition from scribing (with the sleeping scribe) to the printing press. As we've seen in the course, it represented such a huge turning point in communication history, and provides a clearest examples in the ride of how communication advances shape society, not just technology. If I were adding something, I would expand the later digital section a bit more to show social media, mobile communication, and the speed of information sharing today. If I were removing anything, I would probably cut back some of the generic futuristic material in favor of more concrete historical connections.

Based on the history of communication, I would say SSE did succeed as an attraction. It may not have stayed perfectly faithful to EPCOT’s original educational ideal, but it still kept the core message intact: human progress is deeply tied to our ability to communicate. Even through all its revisions, that idea seems to have remained central.
