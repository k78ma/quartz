1. Characteristics of ideal op-amp:
	- Infinite input impedance
	- Zero output impedance
	- Infinite open-loop gain
	- Infinite bandwidth
	- Zero internal noise generation

2. 
	*a.* If $R_{0}=200$:
$$
\begin{align}
R_{1} & =\frac{1}{2}R_{0} =100 \\[2ex]
R_{2} & =\frac{1}{4}R_{0}=50 \\[2ex]
R_{3} & =\frac{1}{8}R_{0}=25
\end{align}
$$
	*b.* Max current flows when all resistors are being used (all switches are closed):
$$
\frac{5}{200}+\frac{5}{100}+\frac{5}{50}+\frac{5}{25}=0.375 \text{ A}
$$
	This is more than the acceptable 35 mA, so we need bigger resistors.

3. R-2R only needs $R$ and $2R$, which makes it easier to fabricate. Binary weighted resistors require a large range of resistor values, which are hard to fabricate

4. For both converters, the accuracy is $0.0005\times 10=5\text{ mV}$
	For 10 bits, we have:
$$
\begin{align}
1 \text{ LSB} & =\frac{10\text{ V}}{2^{10}-1}=9.8\text{ mV}\\[2ex]
\text{DNL} & = \frac{5}{9.8}=0.512 \text{ LSB}
\end{align}
$$
	For 12 bits, we have:
$$
\begin{align}
1 \text{ LSB} & =\frac{10\text{ V}}{2^{12}-1}=2.4\text{ mV}\\[2ex]
\text{DNL} & = \frac{5}{2.4}=2.048 \text{ LSB}
\end{align}
$$
	Therefore, the 10-bit DAC is a better choice. The 12-bit DAC has DNL > 1, which may cause monotonicity errors.

5. 
	*a.* We have
$$
\begin{align}
 V_{\text{out}} & =k\cdot V_{\text{ref}}\cdot B_{\text{max}} \\[2ex]
V_{\text{ref}} & =\frac{V_{\text{out}}}{k\cdot B_{\text{max}}}=\frac{1.4}{\frac{1}{8}\cdot 7}=\boxed{1.6\text{ V}}
\end{align}
$$
	*b.* We have
$$
\text{LSB}=\frac{\text{FSAR}}{2^{n}-1}=\frac{1.4}{2^{3}-1}=0.2	
$$
	or
$$
\text{LSB}=k\cdot V_{\text{ref}}=\frac{1}{8}\cdot 16=0.2
$$
	*c.* Offset error is $0.1\text{ V}$, as this is what is measured at 000 input.
	*d.* $\text{DNL}=0.3 \text{ V}$ (see iPad page 2, Tutorial 06/26/2024)
	*e.* ILE = 0.3 V (see iPad page 2, Tutorial 06/26/2024)
