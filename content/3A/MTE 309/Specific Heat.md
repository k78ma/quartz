Specific heat is the amount of energy required to raise the temperature of a unit mass of a substance by $1 \degree \text{C}$. This is defined as:
$$
c_{v}=\frac{ \partial u }{ \partial T } \quad \left[ \frac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}\cdot \text{K}} \right]
$$
and we also have
$$
u_{2}-u_{1}=\bar{c_{v}}(T_{2}-T_{1})
$$
The above assumes that internal energy is only a function of temperature, such as an [[Ideal Gas|ideal gas]].

Specific heat is not fixed; it changes as a function of temperature.
