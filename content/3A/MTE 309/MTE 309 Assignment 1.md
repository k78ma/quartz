1. A bicyclist picks up speed on a downhill road due to gravity, which pulls them downward and converts potential energy into kinetic energy, increasing their speed. This does not violate the conservation of energy principle because the total energy remains constant, simply changing from potential to kinetic energy.

2. No, the claim is not true. A cup of cold coffee cannot warm up to 80°C by picking up energy from the surrounding air at 22°C. This process would violate the second law of thermodynamics, which states that heat cannot spontaneously flow from a colder body to a hotter body.

3. The driving force for heat transfer is the temperature difference between two objects or regions. The driving force for electric current is the potential difference (voltage) between two points.

4. Heat transfer is a non-equilibrium phenomenon because it occurs only when there is a temperature difference between two objects or regions. In equilibrium, temperatures are uniform, and no heat transfer occurs since there is no driving force.

5. No, there cannot be any heat transfer between two bodies that are at the same temperature, even if they are at different pressures. Heat transfer is driven by a temperature difference, not a pressure difference.

6. An open system can exchange both energy (heat and work) and matter with its surroundings. Examples include a boiling pot of water and an engine. A closed system can exchange energy (heat and work) but not matter with its surroundings. Examples include a sealed, heated container and a piston-cylinder assembly without leakage. An isolated system cannot exchange either energy or matter with its surroundings. Examples include an insulated, sealed thermos flask and the universe.

7. Extensive depend on the size of a system. They change when the size of the system changes. Examples are mass, volume, and total energy. Intensive properties remain the same regardless of the system's size, such as temperature, pressure, and density.
