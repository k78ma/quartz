Typically, for a given [[Constant Volume Specific Heat|constant volume specific heat]] and [[Constant Pressure Specific Heat|constant pressure specific heat]], the following relationships hold:
$$
\begin{align}
q_{v} & <q_{p} \quad \text{for same } dT \\
\Delta u  & <\Delta h \quad \text{where } dh=du+Pdv \\
c_{v} & <c_{p}
\end{align}
$$
## Specific Heat Ratio
The specific heat ratio of a substance is:
$$
k=\frac{c_{p}}{c_{v}}
$$
## Ideal Gas
For an ideal gas, we have:
$$
h=u+Pv=u+RT
$$
thus, enthalpy of an ideal gas is a function of temperature such that $h=h(T)$.

At constant pressure, we have:
$$
c_{p}=\frac{dh}{dT}=\frac{du}{dT}+R
$$
which leads to
$$
\boxed{c_{p}=c_{v}+R}
$$
## Solids and Liquids
For incompressible solids and liquids, we have
$$
\begin{align}
h & =u+Pv \\
dh & =du+P\,dv \\
dh & =du \\
\end{align}
$$
which means that
$$
\boxed{c_{p} = c_{v}=c}
$$
