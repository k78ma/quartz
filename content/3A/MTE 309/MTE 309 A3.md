## Concept Questions

1. Yes, the contents of a propane tank filled with a mixture of liquid and vapor propane can be considered a pure substance. This is because both the liquid and vapor phases consist of the same chemical component (propane), just in different phases.

2. No, there is no difference in the intensive properties of saturated vapor at a given temperature ($T$) and pressure ($P$) and the vapor phase of a saturated mixture at the same $T$ and $P$. Both are characterized by the same temperature, pressure, and other intensive properties.

3. Temperature and pressure are dependent properties in the saturated mixture region because the state of the mixture (amount of liquid and vapor) is fixed by either temperature or pressure. For a given substance in the saturated mixture region, specifying either the temperature or the pressure determines the other property due to the phase equilibrium between liquid and vapor.

4. Boiling process at supercritical pressures differs from subcritical pressures in that above the critical pressure, the distinction between liquid and vapor phases disappears. At supercritical conditions, there is no phase change from liquid to vapor via boiling; instead, the substance continuously transitions from a fluid-like state to a gas-like state without a distinct phase change.

5. The sticking of a perfectly fitting pot and lid after cooking occurs due to the cooling down of the pot, which reduces the internal pressure as the steam condenses. This creates a vacuum effect, making the lid hard to open. To open the lid, you can gently heat the pot to increase the internal pressure, or run the lid under hot water to expand it slightly and equalize the pressure.

6. The reference point selected for the properties of a substance does not affect the thermodynamic analysis, because thermodynamics deals with changes in properties rather than their absolute values. Calculations are based on differences in properties, so the reference point can be chosen for convenience without impacting the outcome of the analysis.

7. The physical significance of $h_{fg}$ (enthalpy of vaporization) represents the energy required to change a unit mass of a substance from liquid to vapor at constant temperature and pressure. It can be obtained from the knowledge of $h_{f}$ (enthalpy of the liquid phase) and $h_{g}$ (enthalpy of the vapor phase) as  $h_{fg} = h_g - h_f$. $h_{fg}$ typically decreases with an increase in pressure and approaches zero as the critical point is approached.

8. Quality in thermodynamics refers to the ratio of the mass of vapor in a saturated mixture to the total mass of the mixture. It is only defined in the two-phase region (saturated liquid-vapor mixture) and has no meaning in the superheated vapor region, where the substance exists only as vapor with no liquid present.
