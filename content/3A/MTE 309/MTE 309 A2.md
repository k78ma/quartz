## Concept Questions
1. **What is the difference between the macroscopic and microscopic forms of energy?** 
The macroscopic form of energy refers to the energy associated with the overall motion and position of an object, such as kinetic and potential energy observable at a large scale. The microscopic form of energy involves the energy at the molecular or atomic level, including thermal energy, chemical energy, and nuclear energy, which are not directly observable without specialized equipment.

2. **What is total energy? Identify the different forms of energy that constitute total energy.** 
Total energy is the sum of all forms of energy present in a system. It includes both macroscopic and microscopic forms of energy. Some different forms of energy that constitute total energy are: Kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical, electrical, magnetic, etc.

3. **How are heat, internal energy, and thermal energy related to each other?** 
Thermal energy is energy that is associated specifically with the random motion of particles within a system, which makes it a part of internal energy (total energy contained within a system). Heat is the transfer of thermal energy between systems due to a temperature difference.

4. **Regarding internal energy: what is the difference between “sensible energy” and “latent energy”?** 
Sensible energy is the portion of internal energy associated with temperature changes in a substance, while latent energy is associated with phase change.

5. **In what forms can energy cross the boundary of a closed-system?** 
Energy can be transferred in the forms of heat or work.

6. **When is the energy crossing the boundary of a closed-system heat? When is it work?** 
Energy crossing the boundary is considered heat when it is transferred due to a temperature difference between the system and its surroundings. Its is considered work when it involves any force acting over a distance.

7. **What are the different mechanisms for transferring energy to or from a control volume?**
Energy can be transferred in the form of heat due to a temperature difference between the control volume and its surroundings (conduction, convection, radiation). Energy transfer can also occur due to mechanical work done by or on the control volume. Mass flow entering or exiting the control volume may also have energy (kinetic or potential) associated with it.
