The total extension or contraction of a uniform bar in pure tension or compression is given by:
$$
\delta=\frac{Fl}{AE}
$$
If we have $\delta=y$ and use the equation $k=\frac{F}{y}$, the spring constant of an axially loaded bar is:
$$
k=\frac{AE}{l}
$$
where:
- $k$ is the spring constant
- $A$ is cross-sectional area
- $\delta$ and $y$ are deformation
- $E$ is modulus of elasticity
- $l$ is the initial length of the bar
- $F$ is axial load of tension or compression

The angular deflection of a uniform solid or hollow round bar subjected to a twisting moment $T$ is
$$
\theta=\frac{Tl}{GJ}= \frac{T}{k}
$$
This can be re-arranged to get:
$$
k=\frac{T}{\theta}=\frac{GJ}{l}
$$
We can also write $J=\pi d^{4} / 32$ for a round bar and convert to radians to get:
$$
\theta=\frac{583.6Tl}{Gd^{4}}
$$
