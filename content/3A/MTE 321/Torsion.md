Any moment vector that is collinear with an axis of a mechanical part is called a *torque vector*, because the moment causes the part to be twisted about that axis. A bar subjected to such a moment is said to be in *torsion*.

![[Torsion.png|456]]

## Angle of Twist
The angle of twist, in radians for a solid round bar is:
$$
\theta=\frac{Tl}{GJ} \quad \text{(radians)}
$$
where:
- $T$ is torque
- $l$ is length
- $G$ is the modulus of rigidity
- $J$ is the [[Polar Moment of Inertia|polar moment of inertia]]

## Shear Stress
Shear stress develop throughout the cross section. For a round bar in torsion, these stresses are given by
$$
\tau=\frac{T\rho}{J}
$$
Designating $r$ as the radius of the outer surface, we have
$$
\tau_{\text{max}}=\frac{Tr}{J}
$$
