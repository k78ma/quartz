The fundamental law of the motor is
$$
F=Bli
$$
where $F$ is the electromagnetic force, $B$ is the magnetic flux density, $l$ is the length of the conductor within the magnetic field, and $i$ is the current.

The EMF induced in the moving conductor is given by
$$
\text{EMF}=Blv
$$
where $v$ is the velocity of the conductor within the magnetic.

In DC motos, torque $T$ and EMF $E$ can be expressed as
$$
\begin{align}
T=k_{t}i_{a} \\
E=k_{e}\omega
\end{align}
$$
where $k_{t}$ is the torque constant, $i_{a}$ is the EMF constant, $i_{a}$ is the armature current and $\omega$ is the angular speed.

Notably, in DC motors, the torque constant $k_{t}$ and the EMF constant $k_{e}$ are equal, implying that their units are also equivalent:
$$
\left[ \frac{\text{Nm}}{\text{A}} \right]=\left[ \frac{\text{V}}{\text{rad/s}} \right]
$$
