Magnetic field generation for electric motors when the magnetic flux is generated by a field coil rather than a permanent field magnet is given by the Magnetic Ohm's law:
$$
\phi=\frac{\text{MMF}}{\mathcal{R}}
$$
where $\text{MMF}=NI$, $\mathcal{R}=\frac{g}{A\mu_{0}}$. $\mathcal{R}$ is [[Magnetic Reluctance|magnetic reluctance]]. $\phi$ is the magnetic flux (conceptually analogous to current).

Magnetic field strength is given by
$$
B=\frac{\phi}{A}=\frac{\mu_{0}NI}{g}
$$
The unit for $B$ is Tesla.

Thus, we can see that the generated electromagnetic field is directly proportional the current flowing through the coil. As current increases, the magnetic flux density also increases, resulting in a stronger magnetic field.
