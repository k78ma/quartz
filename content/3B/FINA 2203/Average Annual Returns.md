Suppose a stock has the following annual returns in the past years:

![[Realized Returns-20250517133604310.png]]

> [!definition] Arithmetic average
> $$
> R_{A} = \frac{4.9+15.8+5.5-37.0+26.5}{5}=3.14\%
> $$
> - Answers the question: "What was your return in an average year over a particular period?"
> - Purpose: To estimate expected return for each year in the future
> - If we have to predict what will be the rate of return next year, the best prediction would be $R_{A}$ return.

> [!definition] Geometric average
> $$
> R_G = ((1.049)(1.158)(1.055)(0.63)(1.265))^{\frac{1}{5}}-1 = 0.4231\%
> $$
> - Answers the question: "What was your return in an average year over a particular period?"
> - Purpose: To measure historical performance
> - If we had invested 100 dollars two years ago, your portfolio balance will be now $100(1+R_{G})^{2}$

We can use the two definitions above to answer the following questions:
1. Suppose you have invested $10,000 in this stock at the beginning of 2005, how much will your investment be worth at the end of 2009?
$$
10,000 \times(1.004231)^{5}=10,213
$$
2. Suppose you plan to invest $10,000 in this stock for the next year, and you assume probabilities of having each of the past 5 years’ returns as equal. What will be the expected payoff of your investment of 10,000 next year?
$$
10,000 \times(1.0314)=10,314
$$
