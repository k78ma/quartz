We saw that [[Stock Prices and Returns|stock valuation]] is determined in terms of the expected future dividends the firm will pay. Estimating these dividends is difficult; a commonly used approximation is to assume that in the long run, dividends will grow at a constant rate $g$. 

This leads to the following timeline:

![[Constant Dividend Growth Model-20250516233152891.png|620]]

Because the expected dividends are a [[Growing Perpetuity|growing perpetuity]], we can calculate their value as
$$
P_{0}=\frac{Div_{1}}{r_{E}-g}
$$


> [!question] Example Problem
> AT&T plans to pay $1.44 per share in dividends in the coming year. Its equity cost of capital is 8%. Dividends are expected to grow by 4% per year in the future. Estimate the value of AT&T’s stock.

Timeline:

![[Constant Dividend Growth Model-20250516233349292.png]]

We have:
$$
P_{0}=\frac{Div_{1}}{r_{E}-g}=\frac{1.44}{0.08-0.04}=36.00
$$
Thus, we would pay 25 times this year's dividend of 1.44 to own AT&T stocks because we are buying claim to this year's dividend and to an infinite growing series of future dividends.

Note that:
- If the question says the company **paid** a dividend of $X last year, this is $D_{0}$.
- If the question says the company **will pay** a dividend $X this year, this will be $D_1$.
- If the question says "the company expects the learnings this year to be $X and the pay out to be Y%", then they will be $E_{1}$, and we can assume **this year** is "**at the end of this year**".
