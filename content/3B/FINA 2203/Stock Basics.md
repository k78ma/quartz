The ownership of a corporation is divided into shares of stock. A public corporation has many owners and its shares trade on a stock market that provides liquidity for a company's shares and determines the market price for those shares.

**Common stock** means a share of ownership in the corporation gives its owners rights to any common divides as well as rights to vote. Generally, corporations issue common stock to raise capital to expand the business. With common stock, there is no need to repay stockholders who bought the stocks – dividends are not mandatory.

On the other hand, investors buy common stock hoping that the earnings of the company will grow and the share price will increase, and receive dividends in the form of cash or additional shares.
