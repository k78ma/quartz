## Density
The density of a fluid, $\rho$, is defined by mass per unit volume:
$$
\rho=\frac{m}{\forall } \equiv \frac{M}{L^{3}} \quad \left[ \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^{3}} \right]
$$
In standard conditions:
- $\rho_{\text{water}}=1000 \text{ kg} / \text{m}^{3}$
- $\rho_{\text{air}}=1.230 \text{ kg} / \text{m}^{3}$

## Specific volume
The specific volume is the volume per unit mass and is defined as:
$$
v=\frac{1}{\rho}= \frac{\forall  }{m}
$$
such that $\rho v=1$.
## Specific weight
The specific weight of a fluid is defined as the weight per unit volume:
$$
\gamma= \frac{mg}{\forall  }=\rho g \quad [\text{N} / \text{m}^{3}]
$$
where $g$ is the local acceleration by gravity, $g=9.81 \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^{2}}$.

## Specific gravity
The specific gravity of a fluid, $SG$, is defined as the ratio of the density of the fluid to the density of water at some specified temperature, usually $4\degree \text{C}$. At this point, $\rho_{\text{H}_{2}\text{O}@4\degree  \text{C}}=1000 \text{ kg}/\text{m}^{3}$.
$$
SG=\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\text{H}_{2}\text{O}@4\degree  \text{C}}}
$$
Since this is a ratio of quantities, it is dimensionless. 

We can use specific gravity to calculate the density of a substance. For example, given $SG_{\text{Hg}}=13.55$, we can calculate
$$
\rho_{\text{Hg}}=13.55 \cdot  \rho_{\text{H}_{2}\text{O}@4\degree  \text{C}} =1.355 \times 10^{4} \text{ kg} / \text{m}^{3}
$$
