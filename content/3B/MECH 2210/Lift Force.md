Lift force arises primarily due to the pressure difference across the surface of an object as it moves through a fluid. Viscous effects, such as shear stress, are secondary in the creation of lift.

Lift force $F_{L}$ is defined as
$$
F_L = C_L \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 A
$$
where $C_{L}$ is the lift coefficient, $\rho$ is the density of the fluid, and $A$ is the reference area.

The lift coefficient $C_{L}$ is influenced by several factors:
$$
C_L = C_L \left(\text{Re}, \frac{\epsilon}{L}, \text{Ma}, \text{Fr}, \text{Shape} \right)
$$
- Re – Reynolds number, representing the ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces
- $\frac{\epsilon}{L}$ – Relative surface roughness to characteristic length
- Ma – Mach number ($V/c$), the ratio of velocity to the speed of sound.
- Fr – Froude number ($V/\sqrt{gL}$), representing inertial forces to gravitational forces
- Shape – The aerodynamic design of the airfoil or object
