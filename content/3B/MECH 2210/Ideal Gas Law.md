The ideal gas law is an [[Equation of State|equation of state]] stating:
$$
p=\rho'RT
$$
where:
- $\rho'$ is the number density
- $R$ is the gas constant $R=N_{A}k_B$
    - $N_{A}=6.023\times 10^{23}$ (Avogadro's number)
    - $k_{B}=1.38 \times 10^{-23} \text{J} / \text{K}$
- $p$ is pressure given by $p \equiv \frac{F}{L^{2}}$.

Note that the pressure $p$ can be expressed in 2 ways:
1. Absolute pressure $p_{\text{abs}}$, taken relative to vacuum $p_{\text{abs}}=0$. The standard sea-level atmospheric pressure is $p_{\text{abs}}=1.013\times 10^{5} \text{ Pa}$
2. Gage pressure $p$ is given relative to the local atmospheric pressure: $p_{\text{abs}}=p+1.013\times 10^{5} \text{ Pa}$
