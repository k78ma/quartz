In my second year, I walked out of my first final exam to an email saying my internship, set to begin in two weeks, had been rescinded in company-wide cuts. Beyond losing an exciting project, I suddenly risked delaying graduation, because Waterloo requires five internships.

After the initial shock, I focused on what I could control. Over the next few days I built a personal website and portfolio, sent dozens of cold emails, posted on LinkedIn, and met with anyone willing to talk. A professor I had previously worked with responded and offered to host me for a research term. What began as a backup plan became a formative experience that clarified my academic interests and built core research skills.

I left that term with a different mindset: many outcomes are outside my control, but I am responsible for how I respond. Acting with agency, advocating for myself, and staying adaptable under pressure now shape how I approach research, engineering, and life.
