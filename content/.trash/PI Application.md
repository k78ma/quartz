**Why do you want to work at Physical Intelligence?**
I'm a big fan of approaches to autonomy that emphasize generality, which seems to be central to π's mission. I think that while many robots have shown great capabilities in narrow domain-specific problems; for example, I previously worked on autonomy for industrial vehicles and warehouse robotic arms, both of which take advantage of overfitting to a narrow problem space. To me, general-purpose robotics presents a far more interesting and meaningful problem that I would really want to work on, and, in the long term, will be much more useful to humanity.

Given that there I don't have a ton of information about π does, another important factor for me is the team. Having worked at research labs, startups, and big companies, I think a startup like π is ideal. More importantly, the team at π has many people that I admire and would love to work with; specifically, Dr. Chelsea Finn and Dr. Sergey Levine have written several of my favorite papers, and their research goals seem to align very well with my interests above. Especially since this is an internship position, one of my priorities is to learn as much as possible, and π seems like a great place to do so in this regard. 

**What would you most like to work on?** 
At this stage, it's a bit hard to define what I want to work on since I don't have enough any specifics of what π does or needs. That being said, I feel that I thrive in roles that involve solve open-ended problems - these excites me the most, and thus results in the highest quality output. This includes both research and engineering problems. 

Some specific research interests at the moment are self-supervised learning and effective world modeling. How can we train robots on new tasks with as little data (preferably unlabeled) as possible? What representations of the world around us are most conducive to effectively learning physical cause-and-effect?

Some specific engineering challenges that I think fit the description above and would be interesting to work on at π are how to build a system that is easily adaptable to different types of robots. How do we make it easy to train models for different robots? How do we make deployment easy, scalable, and reproducible? 

**What're some examples of your extraordinary accomplishments?**
A large technical undertaking that I'm proud of is my work at Polymath Robotics, a startup working on autonomy for industrial vehicles. Since the Polymath team is very small, I essentially built the entire ML stack from scratch, including a tool for custom LiDAR data annotation, implementations of models, training/fine-tuning code (including distributed training), quantization for speedups on limited hardware, and an API for cloud deployment as well.
