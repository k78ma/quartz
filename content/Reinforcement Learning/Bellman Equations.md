We may not know the [[Value Function|state values]] $v[s_{t}]$ or [[Value Function|action values]] $q[s_{t}, a_{t}]$ for any policy. However, we know that they must be consistent with one another, and it's easy to write relations between these quantities. The state values $v[s_{t}]$ can be found by taking a weighted sum of the action values $q[s_{t}, a_{t}]$, where the weights depend on the probability under the policy $\pi[a_{t}|s_{t}]$ of taking that action:
$$
v[s_{t}] = \sum_{a_{t}}\pi[a_{t}|s_{t}]q[s_{t}, a_{t}]
$$

![[Expected Return-1787111343415.webp]]

Similarly, the value of an action is the intermediate reward $r_{t+1}=r[s_{t}, a_{t}]$ generated by taking the action, plus the value $v[s_{t+1}]$ of being in the subsequent state $s_{t+1}$ discounted by $\gamma$. Since the assignment $s_{t+1}$ is not deterministic, we weight the values $v[s_{t+1}]$ according to the transition $Pr(s_{t+1}|s_{t}, a_{t})$:
$$
q[s_{t}, a_{t}] = r[s_{t}, a_{t}] + \gamma \cdot \sum_{s_{t+1}}Pr(s_{t+1}|s_{t}, a_{t})v[s_{t+1}]
$$

![[Expected Return-1787112312642.webp]]

Substituting this equation into the state value equation above gives a relation between the state value at time $t$ and $t+1$:
$$
v[s_{t}] = \sum_{a_{t}}\pi[a_{t}|s_{t}]\left( r[s_{t}, a_{t}]+\gamma \cdot \sum_{s_{t+1}} Pr(s_{t+1}|s_{t}, a_{t}) v[s_{t+1}]  \right)
$$
Substituting the $v[s_{t}]$ equation into the $q[s_{t}, a_{t}]$ equation gives a relation between the action value at time $t$ and $t+1$:
$$
q[s_{t}, a_{t}] = r[s_{t}, a_{t}] + \gamma \cdot \sum_{s_{t+1}}Pr(s_{t+1}|s_{t}, a_{t})\left( \sum_{a_{t+1}} \pi[a_{t+1}|s_{t+1}]q[s_{t+1}, a_{t+1}] \right)
$$
These two relations are the *Bellman equations* and are the backbone of many RL methods. In short, they say that the state (action) values have to be self-consistent. Consequently, when we update an estimate of one state (action) value, this will have a ripple effect that causes modifications to all the others.
